# Alignment Model

Alignment means every team can explain how its OKRs support the organization's strategy, and leadership can see where the strategy has no team behind it. This model keeps that connection visible without turning OKRs into a copy-and-paste cascade.

---

## Three Levels

| Level | Owns | Time horizon | Typical count |
|-------|------|--------------|---------------|
| Company or organization | Strategic priorities | Annual, reviewed quarterly | 3 to 5 priorities |
| Department or product area | Objectives that advance a priority | Quarterly | 2 to 4 objectives |
| Team | Objectives and Key Results | Quarterly | 1 to 3 objectives |

---

## How Direction and Commitment Flow

**Direction flows down.** Leadership sets the priorities and explains the reasoning behind them.

**Commitment flows up.** Teams propose their own objectives and Key Results in response. The people closest to the work know best what can be measured and moved.

**Negotiation happens in the middle.** Department leaders reconcile what teams propose with what the priorities need, then close the gaps.

Avoid handing teams finished Key Results. A team that did not write its Key Result will track it, but will not own it.

---

## Alignment Rules

1. Every team objective names the priority it serves. If it serves none, it is either keep-the-lights-on work or a signal that the strategy is missing something.
2. Not every team needs to support every priority. Forced coverage produces weak OKRs.
3. A parent's Key Result can become a child's objective, but only when the child team controls the outcome.
4. When two teams depend on each other for one result, write a shared Key Result with one owner and name the contributing team.
5. Reserve capacity for work that is not in the OKRs. A common starting point is [20 to 30%] for operations, support, and maintenance. Adjust the range to your context.

## Reading the Map

| What you see | What it means | What to do |
|--------------|---------------|------------|
| A priority with no team objectives under it | The strategy has no execution behind it | Assign it or remove it from the priority list |
| Many teams under one priority, few under others | Capacity is concentrated | Confirm that this matches intent |
| A team objective with no priority | Unaligned work or a missing priority | Decide which, then act on it |
| The same dependency named by several teams | A bottleneck | Give that team a Key Result for it, or resequence |

---

## Running an Alignment Review

Hold this once per quarter, after teams draft OKRs and before they are final.

1. Each team shares its draft objectives and the priority each one serves.
2. The facilitator fills in the alignment map live.
3. The group reads the map using the table above.
4. Gaps, overlaps, and dependencies get an owner and a date.
5. Teams revise and publish final OKRs within one week.
