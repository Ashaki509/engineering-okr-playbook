# OKR Anti-Patterns

These are the common ways an OKR practice turns into a reporting exercise. Each entry lists how to spot the pattern and what to do about it.

---

## Writing Anti-Patterns

| Anti-pattern | How to spot it | Fix |
|--------------|----------------|-----|
| The task list | Key Results start with "launch," "complete," "deliver," or "migrate" | Ask "so that what?" and measure the answer |
| No baseline | A target with no starting number | Pull the baseline, or make baselining the first Key Result |
| The binary | Done or not done, with nothing in between | Find a metric that moves gradually, so progress shows before the deadline |
| Too many | More than 3 objectives or more than 9 Key Results per team | Cut until the list fits on one screen |
| The vanity metric | A number that goes up without changing a decision | Choose the metric a customer or the business would notice |
| No owner | A team name where a person should be | Assign one person who reports the number |

---

## Planning Anti-Patterns

| Anti-pattern | How to spot it | Fix |
|--------------|----------------|-----|
| Copy-paste cascade | Team OKRs repeat the department's wording | Teams write their own in response to the priorities |
| Sandbagging | Every Key Result finishes at 100% every quarter | Separate committed targets from stretch targets and label each |
| Everything is an OKR | Routine operations written as objectives | Keep operational work in health metrics, outside the OKRs |
| Planning in isolation | Dependencies found in week 6 | Run an alignment review before OKRs are final |
| Late start | OKRs published three weeks into the quarter | Begin planning four weeks before the quarter opens |

---

## Tracking Anti-Patterns

| Anti-pattern | How to spot it | Fix |
|--------------|----------------|-----|
| Set and forget | No updates between planning and the end-of-quarter review | Hold a weekly check-in with a confidence rating |
| Watermelon status | Green all quarter, red in the final week | Report confidence, and ask what would have to be true to hit the target |
| Status theater | Check-ins spent reading numbers aloud | Share numbers in writing first and use the meeting for decisions |
| Frozen OKRs | Circumstances changed and the OKR did not | Allow mid-quarter changes with a written reason and leader approval |

---

## Culture Anti-Patterns

| Anti-pattern | How to spot it | Fix |
|--------------|----------------|-----|
| OKRs as performance reviews | Scores feed ratings or compensation | Keep them separate, or teams will stop setting ambitious targets |
| Punishing red | Teams hide risk until it is too late to help | Treat an early red status as useful information and respond with support |
| No retrospective | The same misses repeat each quarter | Close every quarter with a review that produces changes |

---

## Quick Self-Check

Answer yes or no for your current quarter.

- [ ] Could a new team member explain our objectives after reading them once?
- [ ] Does every Key Result have a baseline, a target, and one owner?
- [ ] Did any Key Result change status in the last two weeks?
- [ ] Did the last check-in produce at least one decision?
- [ ] Did last quarter's review change how we planned this quarter?

Two or more "no" answers suggest the practice has drifted toward reporting. Pick the matching anti-pattern above and fix that one first.
